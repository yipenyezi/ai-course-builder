## Method qualifications

Needs assessment versus needs analysis terminology varies across sources; this project uses “needs analysis” for the combined investigation of the gap, its possible causes, and appropriate intervention. The five lenses need not be strictly sequential. Early task and learner context informs diagnosis; detailed SME workflow/content analysis follows a training decision and may reopen it.

The source texts use Gilbert-style environmental and person-side categories. Here they are prompts for investigation, not a validated diagnosis of an individual's motivation/capacity. A mixed-cause problem needs mixed solutions. Training can support knowledge, skill, judgment, and practiced behaviors, but cannot by itself remove absent tools, contradictory priorities, or punitive management conditions.

Past success, self-report, or confidence alone cannot establish current independent competence. Learner preferences are not fixed learning styles. A knowledge gap may be served by a job aid rather than memorization; a formal content mandate is not evidence of likely performance impact. Stakeholder-imposed content may be documented as a constraint without misrepresenting it as a validated training need.

Plan review of results at the beginning. Be explicit about baseline quality, access limitations, competing influences, and responsibility. AI assists analysis and can be wrong; stakeholders validate evidence and decisions. Production approval gates and learner-data handling remain governed by the project workflow and evaluation specifications.
