# Needs and performance analysis

Diagnose before recommending a course. Use the [analysis template](references/analysis-template.md) to collect inputs and deliver a reviewable brief. Read [source and interpretation notes](references/source-notes.md) when reconciling methods or rating claims. All artifacts and examples are English unless explicitly requested otherwise. This skill is not yet invoked by the app.

## Gather what matters

Start with existing materials and decisions; do not ask the ID to re-enter confirmed information. Identify the business problem, requester, sponsor, affected role/tasks, expected performance, actual evidence, urgency, constraints, and intended measure of success. A request for a course describes a proposed solution, not an established need.

Investigate five connected lenses: organizational/business need, performance gap, learners, tasks, and environment. Include technical delivery constraints within environment. Scope depth to uncertainty, consequences, access, and project resources; collect enough learner and task context to assess the intervention, then deepen task analysis in the SME workflow interview after the decision.

Seek perspectives from business stakeholders, process owners/SMEs, and actual performers. Choose interviews, observation, work samples, records, surveys, or focus groups to answer specific uncertainties. Record source, period, sample/coverage, definitions, limitations, and contradictions. Qualitative evidence can be useful; do not turn anecdotes into prevalence estimates or survey preferences into demonstrated competence.

## Diagnose and compare options

1. Express expected and actual task performance on comparable terms. Preserve units, denominators, periods, and conditions; do not subtract incompatible measures or invent missing baselines/targets. Distinguish new/future capability requirements from demonstrated current deficits.
2. Map business goal → observable action → necessary knowledge/skill. Replace broad behavior labels such as “understand customers” with actions such as eliciting and confirming the intended outcome. Link evidence and gap IDs.
3. Investigate information/feedback, tools/resources/access/time, workflow/management/support, incentives/priorities, and knowledge/skill. Treat causes as supported, hypothesized, contradicted, or unknown; record how hypotheses could be tested. Success once under ideal conditions does not rule out a skill/fluency problem. Self-report and correlation alone do not establish cause. Do not infer a named person's motivation, capacity, or attitude from poor results.
4. Match interventions to causes and compare feasible alternatives: practice/coaching, job aids/reference, process/tool/access fixes, feedback, manager support, incentive changes, or combinations. A knowledge gap does not automatically require a course. Keep tradeoffs and dependencies explicit; do not invent costs or promise the cheapest solution without evidence.
5. Prioritize tasks using consequences/criticality, frequency, difficulty, affected scope, and feasibility with stakeholder input. A rare high-consequence task can be a priority. No automatic scoring formula or threshold is assumed.
6. Recommend one outcome: `training`, `non_training`, `combined`, or `insufficient_evidence`. State the basis, residual uncertainty, intervention owners, and what training alone cannot fix. For insufficient evidence, deliver a focused investigation plan, not final objectives or a production storyboard.

## Review gate and handoff

Present the brief, cause/intervention table, learner brief, action-to-capability matrix, and unresolved decisions together. The ID/sponsor confirms the intervention; the SME validates task facts. Approval records identify a version and actual reviewer. Never invent sign-off or use stakeholder insistence as evidence that training will work.

For `training` or `combined`, hand approved gaps, candidate actions, sources, learner/environment constraints, and non-training dependencies to the SME interview/content-selection stage in `docs/course-design-template.md`. Final SMART objectives follow workflow and scope confirmation. Do not skip that stage just because the analysis matrix names skills.

For `non_training`, deliver actionable recommendations and a follow-up measure; do not force course creation. If stakeholders require content despite unresolved analysis, record it as a constrained request with risks and missing evidence, not a confirmed diagnosis; the current production gate remains closed until its requirements are met.

Plan Kirkpatrick evaluation from intended Level 4 results back to Level 3 behaviors, Level 2 learning, and Level 1 relevance/experience. Confirm baseline, source, timing, owner, and access early; missing measures remain pending. Completion or simulation scores do not prove workplace change or causal business impact. Use `docs/evaluation-and-tracking.md` for data decisions.

## Completion checks

- Claims trace to sources; assumptions, conflicting evidence, and unknowns remain visible.
- Learner groups and work/learning conditions inform recommendations without stereotypes or fixed learning-style labels.
- Every recommended intervention addresses a supported cause or is clearly a test of a hypothesis.
- Ratings have explicit anchors, required level, evidence basis, and reviewer; no unsupported score-to-training mapping.
- No final training design is passed downstream on an unconfirmed diagnosis.
- Results include stakeholder decisions and a way to revisit whether the selected intervention worked.
