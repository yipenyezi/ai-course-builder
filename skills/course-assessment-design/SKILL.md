# Course assessment design

Create evidence of the intended learning, not a test of guessing strategies or unnecessarily difficult reading. Work from approved objectives, selected content, SME workflow/mistakes, and source materials. These are project-local instructions; the app does not yet invoke this skill automatically.

Read [item-writing guidance](references/item-writing.md) for format-specific rules, source limitations, and review criteria. Use [output contract and example](references/output-contract.md) when producing a brief, items, or review report. Project paths below are relative to the repository root.

## Confirm the brief first

Confirm with the ID: assessment purpose and intended interpretation; learners/prerequisites; exact objective aspects and cognitive demands; item count per aspect; format; real-life versus synthetic scenarios; approved facts and allowed references; feedback/answer-release timing; attempts, hints, scoring/partial credit if used; accessibility and authoring tool.

Propose a blueprint and surface missing choices together. Explicitly distinguish proposed defaults from confirmed requirements. Do not silently invent counts, pass marks, time limits, or require two questions per objective. Existing confirmation persists across turns. If a requested count cannot cover the intended evidence, explain the gap and propose alternatives. An illustrative draft may proceed with clearly marked assumptions, but must not be presented as an approved item set.

## Design evidence, then write

1. Link every item to one primary objective aspect, work decision/behavior, and approved content/source. Match the required thinking, not just the verb label. A decision MCQ may assess judgment but cannot by itself establish ability to conduct a complete conversation or operate a tool.
2. Select the format that elicits the needed evidence. Use a realistic scenario for application when appropriate. A decorative story around a definition does not make it application. Use a new but comparable situation for independent assessment rather than copying an answered practice example.
3. Write a focused task with enough information to resolve it. For single-best-answer items, apply the cover-up test: a knowledgeable learner should understand the problem and formulate an answer without seeing the options. Assess one central problem; move separate decisions into separately identified items.
4. Establish the key and source-based rationale, then build plausible distractors from common mistakes. Label proposed mistakes for SME confirmation. Keep choices homogeneous, compatible with the stem, mutually distinguishable, and comparable in length/style. Use fewer plausible choices rather than padding the set with absurd ones.
5. Provide explanatory feedback per option or rubric criterion, with source/job-aid references and a useful next step. For scenarios, distinguish the fictional consequence from the explanation. Respect confirmed release timing; do not expose answers through hints or later items before submission.
6. For constructed responses, specify acceptable variation, required evidence, performance anchors, and partial-credit rules if approved. Do not require exact model wording or penalize grammar unless it is part of the objective. Calibrate ambiguous and alternative answers with the SME.

## Keep practice and live role-play distinct

Early guided sales practice can have fixed choices and choice-specific feedback. Later AI role-play is completely free-form: no options or unsolicited suggested replies. Apply `docs/role-play-criteria-template.md`; the counterpart stays in character, evaluation stays private until the final debrief, and the separate coach gives hints only on request. Final feedback cites actual learner contributions and full context. Do not retrofit a multiple-choice answer key onto open conversation.

## Review individual items and the full set

- Check factual accuracy, sufficient cues, one defensible best answer, distractor reasoning, and alignment. Flag uncertain facts for SME review rather than silently changing the key.
- Check clear instructions, reading burden, essential/readable graphics with accessible equivalents, bias, and irrelevant cultural assumptions.
- Detect grammatical/key-word clues, convergence cues, answer-length patterns, absolute wording giveaways, overlapping choices, unnecessary negatives, and all/none-of-the-above or combination options.
- Verify coverage/counts against the confirmed blueprint. Review cross-item answer leakage and accidental dependencies. A linked case may share context, but avoid repeatedly penalizing an initial error; specify recovery or provide necessary state for each decision.
- Vary correct-answer positions without a predictable cycle; retain meaningful numeric/chronological ordering where needed. If shuffled, use stable option IDs so keys, feedback, and tracking still match. Do not pad or distort answers merely to equalize lengths or position counts.
- Report blockers, proposed edits, and remaining decisions. Send final items for ID/SME review, save approval against a version, and invalidate affected downstream artifacts after material changes.

## Delivery and evaluation

Return the confirmed/proposed blueprint, item bank, coverage report, and review log using the output contract. Keep learner-facing content separate from keys, rationales, and build notes. Supply Storyline slide or Rise lesson/block references as appropriate; do not imply native tool support without verification.

Passing a writing checklist does not prove validity or reliability. Pilot with representative learners when feasible; inspect response reasoning, ambiguity, distractor use, difficulty, and discrimination when sample/data permit. Flag anomalous results for investigation, not automatic item deletion. No universal statistical cutoffs or certification claims. Course assessment supplies Kirkpatrick Level 2 evidence; Level 3/4 require workplace and business evidence. Align planned xAPI records with `docs/evaluation-and-tracking.md` without leaking keys or hidden rubric content to learners.
