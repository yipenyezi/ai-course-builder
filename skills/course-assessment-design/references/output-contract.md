# Assessment output contract

## 1. Blueprint for ID confirmation

| Objective ID and exact aspect | Work task / gap | Required thinking and evidence | Format | Count | Scenario source/type | Allowed resources | Confirmation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Existing ID | Specific aspect, not entire topic | What the response can establish | Proposed or confirmed | ID-selected | SME/public/synthetic | Approved aids | Proposed / confirmed / unresolved |

Record purpose, learners, intended interpretation, language, authoring target, response directions, timing if needed, scoring/partial credit, attempts, hint availability, feedback/key release, owner and version. Unconfirmed fields stay explicit; illustrative drafts are not final items.

## 2. Item record

- Stable item ID, version, status, objective/aspect, source/content references, and tool slide/block reference.
- Evidence claim and limitation; cognitive demand justified by what the learner does.
- Learner-facing stem/scenario, instructions, essential media description and accessibility equivalent.
- Options with stable IDs (for selected response); separate key, key rationale, distractor misconception/source, consequence if relevant, and explanatory feedback per option.
- For open response: expected evidence, valid alternatives, criterion anchors, and approved scoring rules; model answers illustrate rather than constrain wording.
- Feedback timing, attempts/hint rules, and facts awaiting confirmation.
- SME/ID review decision against version; no fabricated approval.

## 3. Set-level review

Return objective-aspect coverage and actual-versus-requested counts, uncovered aspects, ambiguity/source blockers, length/position/grammar clues, cross-item leakage/dependencies, accessibility/format concerns, and fixes. Distinguish expert review from learner pilot findings and statistical evidence. Identify downstream artifacts needing re-review.

## Original synthetic example: discovery judgment

Status: illustrative, not approved; item count and release rules require ID confirmation.

Objective aspect: choose discovery before recommendation when a customer's underlying problem is not yet understood. This item does not establish ability to conduct the full conversation.

Stem: A retailer asks whether ClearLake can improve inventory forecasting. The seller has not yet explored current performance or the desired business outcome. Which next action best establishes what the customer needs? Select one answer.

- A: Schedule a technical demonstration of forecasting features.
- B: Ask which business outcome better forecasting should improve.
- C: Recommend a migration plan based on the current warehouse.

Key: B. It elicits the business goal before choosing a solution. Its position is illustrative; inspect position/length patterns when assembling the set.

| Option | Proposed mistake or reasoning | Fictional consequence | Feedback |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Premature demonstration; proposed for SME confirmation | The customer asks what the demonstration would address | A demonstration may be appropriate later; first establish the business outcome to determine its scope. |
| B | Explore the goal | The customer describes stockouts and inconsistent inventory data | You elicited a business goal and a possible data issue. Confirm understanding and investigate before recommending. |
| C | Premature solution; proposed for SME confirmation | The customer asks why migration is necessary | Knowing that a warehouse exists does not establish a migration need. Clarify the problem and constraints first. |

Source basis: the repository's explicitly fictional ClearLake case; product capabilities beyond that case must not be inferred. Feedback is authored guided practice, not generated evaluation of a free-form learner response. This example needs expert review and piloting before any measurement claim.
