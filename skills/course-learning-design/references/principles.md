# Learning principles: original synthesis

Source basis: selected passages in the user-supplied editions of Cathy Moore, *Map It* (2017), especially the action-mapping overview and activity-design structure; Peter C. Brown, Henry L. Roediger III, and Mark A. McDaniel, *Make It Stick* (2014), chapters 2–5 and 8. This is a concise design synthesis, not a reproduction or exhaustive review. The books and extraction files must not enter the repository.

| Principle | Design decision | Review evidence / boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Action mapping | Work backward from a meaningful business goal to actions, barriers, practice, and necessary information | Each activity has a work action; non-training causes have appropriate interventions. A course is not assumed to be the solution. |
| Consequence-based practice | Use SME-confirmed decisions with tempting mistakes and realistic outcomes | Learners choose or act before seeing consequences; no implausible distractors or invented policy |
| Retrieval | Ask learners to recall, explain, decide, or perform rather than merely reread | An attempt precedes the answer; open reference remains appropriate for tasks normally performed with a job aid |
| Spacing | Revisit important behaviors after intervals, including beyond the course when feasible | ID approves timing and channel; avoid a universal prescribed schedule |
| Interleaving and variation | Mix related problem types when learners need to discriminate between them; vary surface context while preserving the skill | Provide enough initial support; random topic switching is not a substitute for purposeful practice |
| Generation | Invite an initial explanation or solution before showing a model when it is productive | Give enough cues and corrective feedback; avoid overwhelming novices or inventing time pressure |
| Elaboration and reflection | Ask why a response fits, how it connects to work, and what to do differently next time | Prompt concrete reasoning, not only “How did you feel?” |
| Calibration | Compare confidence/prediction with observed performance and feedback | Fluency, preference, immediate success, and attractive animation do not establish durable learning |

Productive difficulty concerns the target skill; confusing navigation, unreadable text, and unnecessary animation are design defects. Match support to prior knowledge and task difficulty. Do not assign instruction to fixed “visual/auditory learner” categories.

Application example: a synthetic seller first studies a reasoned discovery example, attempts a decision, later conducts an open conversation, and revisits a different customer problem after an ID-selected interval. Evaluate actual questioning and confirmation, not whether the fictional customer agrees to buy.
