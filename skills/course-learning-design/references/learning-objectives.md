# Learning objectives: behavior first

Original synthesis of the user-supplied objective-writing and Bloom excerpts. Treat their sales cases as illustrations, not verified benchmarks. Do not copy their conversion statistics into project evidence.

## Derivation

Start from the approved performance gap and selected work task. Write the terminal objective as what the role will do at work after learning, not what the learner clicks, reads, completes, or memorizes during the course. Identify necessary enabling capabilities by working backward. Include only prerequisites the analysis supports; a job aid may remove the need to memorize details.

Use a concise stem: **By [learning milestone], [role] will be able to [observable action and object].** Add conditions when tools, references, pressure, or context materially change what counts as successful performance. Add explicit criteria when needed to distinguish acceptable from unacceptable performance. Conditions and standards can live in the linked assessment specification rather than making the learner-facing sentence cumbersome.

SMART is a feasibility and alignment check, not a demand for a percentage, stopwatch, or deadline in every sentence. Measurable means observable evidence can be judged against a defensible standard; not every objective needs a numerical metric. Record a justified “not needed in the sentence” decision instead of fabricating a threshold. Essential standards still need ID/SME agreement before assessment approval.

Avoid outcomes outside learner control: customer purchase, customer emotion, business revenue, and injury rates are not direct proof of the learner's behavior. Describe the questioning, recommendation, communication, or safe action the learner controls. Keep organizational outcomes in the evaluation plan.

## Bloom's taxonomy

Use revised Bloom levels to describe the actual thinking required: remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, create. Observable verbs help wording but do not assign a level by themselves. “Select” can be recall, application, or evaluation depending on the task, information, and criteria. Match the job demand; do not maximize the level or require a lesson at every level. Cognitive taxonomy alone does not adequately describe all physical or interpersonal performance.

Separate terminal and enabling objectives with explicit parent links. Enabling objectives must support the terminal behavior. Avoid automatically teaching memorization before every application task; determine what can be looked up and what prerequisite evidence already exists.

### Using the supplied Bloom handout

The user-supplied eight-page *Bloom's Taxonomy* handout contains legacy categories, verb lists, question stems, and assessment examples. Use it as a wording aid, not an automatic classifier. Its attribution is a compilation rather than a fully verified primary-source account. The original PDF remains outside the repository.

| Handout terminology | Revised terminology used in this project | Evidence to elicit |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge | Remember | Retrieve a relevant fact or procedure when recall is actually needed |
| Comprehension | Understand | Explain, interpret, or summarize meaning |
| Application | Apply | Use a procedure or principle in the relevant situation |
| Analysis | Analyze | Distinguish relevant cues, relationships, assumptions, or causes |
| Evaluation | Evaluate | Make and justify a judgment against explicit criteria |
| Synthesis | Create | Produce or organize a suitable new solution or artifact |

This is a terminology bridge, not a claim that the revision merely renamed categories. The handout places synthesis before evaluation; the project uses the revised order with create after evaluate. Do not mix labels/orders within one course map without explanation.

The handout repeats verbs across levels and lists some verbs both as examples and as words to avoid. Resolve this through the complete task and evidence: “recognize” can be assessable when learners identify specified cues; “memorize” describes an internal process, so specify recall/listing or performance evidence instead. “Understand” may name a taxonomy level but is insufficient as a standalone objective behavior.

Use its question stems as prompts for drafting, not mandatory wording. “What do you think?” needs a defined decision and basis for judgment when used for assessment. A role-play is not automatically create/synthesis, and a multiple-choice item is not automatically remember: classify the reasoning and performance actually required. Include only the enabling levels needed for the task, rather than teaching every rung of the taxonomy.

## Objective record and review

Record ID, role, gap/task/source references, terminal/enabling type and parent ID if applicable, formal objective, learner-facing purpose, thinking/performance demand with rationale, conditions/criterion decision, milestone, practice, assessment evidence and limitations, and review status.

Ask: can the behavior be observed; is it meaningful to the work gap; is it achievable in the learning window; does the learner control it; can the proposed assessment elicit it? An observable definition explanation is not enough when the task requires carrying out a conversation. A fluent sentence does not prove a valid diagnosis.

## Synthetic ClearLake example

- Terminal LO1: **By the end of the lesson, sales representatives will be able to clarify a prospective customer's business need before recommending a solution.**
- Enabling LO1a: **Identify information still needed to clarify a customer's business problem.**
- Enabling LO1b: **Use discovery questions to elicit the customer's desired business outcome.**
- Enabling LO1c: **Summarize the customer's stated need to check understanding before recommending a solution.**
- Learner-facing purpose: **Find out what the customer needs before proposing a solution.**
- Evidence: a guided decision can assess LO1a; an open conversation must elicit questioning and confirmation for LO1b/LO1c and the terminal claim. Successful sale is not the criterion. Allowed references and rubric standards require ID confirmation.

These are conditional drafts until the diagnosis and SME scope are approved. They do not authorize new requirements or claim that one lesson assures workplace transfer.
