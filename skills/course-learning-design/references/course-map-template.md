## 2. Finalize feasible SMART objectives

For each objective, record stable ID, gap/task/content references, learner behavior, justified conditions/resources if needed, approved evidence/standard, learning milestone, and ID/SME decision. Use the SMART checks in `docs/course-design-template.md` (repository-relative). Do not invent a pass mark, deadline, or speed requirement.

Check feasibility against prior knowledge, allowed practice time, learner authority, and available tools. An outcome outside learner control is a business result, not a promise of learning mastery. Split unrelated behaviors when different evidence is needed; avoid fragmentation that obscures a meaningful whole task.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [evidence] | [learning or delivery requirement] | [format, support, practice, sequence] | [reasoning] | [feasible option] | [ID decision] |

Conditional examples, not universal prescriptions:

- Limited prerequisite knowledge may warrant a worked example with expert reasoning and supported practice before independent performance.
- Existing knowledge with inconsistent judgment may warrant varied decision cases rather than repeated introductory explanation; verify the actual cause first.
- Work normally performed with a reference should practice finding and using the reference, not automatically require memorization.
- A short learning window may require smaller units and later practice, or reduced scope; it does not justify claiming full mastery in less time.
- No audio access means essential information needs a text/visual path. Low connectivity constrains live AI role-play; propose a feasible authored alternative without claiming equivalence of evidence.
- An environmental cause needs an environmental intervention; do not compensate by adding lessons.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [ID] | [references] | [confirmed or unknown] | [why included] | [reasoning made visible if needed] | [action, conditions, scaffolding] | [timing] | [observable response; what it cannot establish] | [point of work] | [estimate, not task deadline] |

Map sequence dependencies explicitly. Include an opening problem only when it establishes relevant stakes or a learning question. A case may recur with new information and increasingly independent decisions. Do not add a hook, animation, quiz, or role-play solely to satisfy a format quota.

Where appropriate, gradually remove support: worked example → guided decision → less-supported variation → independent performance. This is not mandatory for every audience. Experienced learners may need a different entry path, but skipping requires agreed evidence rather than confidence alone.

For sales, fixed-choice practice can precede a completely free-form AI role-play. Hints remain learner-requested; the counterpart does not reveal the rubric, and formal role-play feedback comes at the final debrief. One MCQ may demonstrate a local decision but not a full discovery conversation.

## 5. Alignment and feasibility review

- Every unit traces to a selected task/objective or an explicitly approved orientation/access need.
- Every objective has a feasible opportunity to practice and appropriate evidence of performance, including relevant exceptions.
- Cognitive demands and performance conditions align; recall cannot stand in for execution without qualification.
- Counts, assessed aspects, formats, scoring, and feedback release are confirmed through the assessment skill before final item generation.
- Prerequisites are taught, supported, or evidenced; later content does not silently assume them.
- Estimates include reading, response, feedback, and retry time; compare total with the available learning window and revisit scope when necessary.
- Tools, accessibility, references, and connectivity match actual conditions; non-training dependencies have owners.
- Workplace follow-up and job aids address application; Kirkpatrick Level 3/4 evidence is not inferred from simulated success.

## 6. Review and storyboard handoff

Record ID decisions against the map/objective version and SME factual confirmations. Transfer approved sequence, objective/task/source IDs, content, practice/assessment brief, feedback policy, job aids, media requirements, and constraints to Rise lesson/block or Storyline slide storyboarding. Visual previews may explore presentation without changing instructional intent. Material upstream changes require review of affected map rows and downstream artifacts.

## Synthetic illustration

If approved evidence supports a discovery skill gap among novice sellers, one candidate sequence is a customer-problem hook, expert reasoning example, guided decision, varied case, and open discovery role-play. The role-play must elicit both questioning and confirmation if the objective requires both. A guided MCQ about the first question alone cannot cover that objective. Product reference material remains available where needed. This is a conditional example, not approval of the repository's still-unresolved ClearLake diagnosis.
