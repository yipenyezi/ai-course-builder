# Course design: guided ID and SME template

Status: specification for planned functionality, not an implemented generator. Use original, public, or synthetic examples in this repository.

Follow the sequence below after performance diagnosis supports training content as part of the intervention. AI drafts and identifies gaps; the ID and SME confirm the workflow, scope, facts, and standards. New interview evidence may reopen the intervention decision.

## 1. Diagnosis handoff

Prepare and approve this handoff using the [needs analysis skill](../skills/course-needs-analysis/SKILL.md) and [analysis template](../skills/course-needs-analysis/references/analysis-template.md). Transfer its shared learner brief and environment constraints rather than independently inventing new audience assumptions.

- Business problem and desired outcome: [reference]
- Performance gaps and data evidence: [IDs, source, scope, limitations]
- Supported training-relevant causes and unresolved hypotheses: [references]
- Intervention decision and approver: [training / combined intervention; rationale]
- Non-training dependencies: [process, tools, resources, owners]

Do not promise that a course can fix an unresolved environmental cause.

## 2. SME workflow interview

Use the [SME analysis skill](../skills/course-sme-analysis/SKILL.md) and its [interview/synthesis template](../skills/course-sme-analysis/references/interview-template.md) to prepare questions, synthesize evidence, and obtain factual/scope review before final objectives.

| Area | Interview prompts | Capture |
|---|---|---|
| Workflow | Walk through the task from trigger to completion. Which tools, inputs, handoffs, and constraints matter? What does successful completion look like? | Ordered steps, dependencies, approved workflow and sources |
| Incidents | Describe a specific incident. What happened, what information was available then, what did the person do, and what happened next? | Incident ID, sequence, stakes, evidence versus recollection |
| Decision points | Where must someone choose? What cues distinguish valid options? What would an expert notice that a novice might miss? | Decision, available cues, alternatives, reasoning |
| Common mistakes | Which tempting shortcuts or misinterpretations occur? Why do people choose them? | Actual mistake, cause, consequence, correction |
| Exceptions | When does the usual process not apply? When should someone stop, look up guidance, or escalate? | Exception, boundary, escalation route |
| Scenario seeds | Which incident could introduce the lesson, recur through it, or test a decision? | Candidate hook, decision points, safe fictionalization |

For each note, record its ID, source/location, confirmation status, and linked task/gap. Missing information becomes a follow-up question. Do not invent incidents and label them as real. For portfolio examples, create synthetic equivalents rather than recording confidential incidents.

## 3. Content selection and scope approval

| Item ID | Task/gap and SME note | Content or action | Category | Reason | Destination | Confirmation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [ID] | [references] | [item] | [must-do / must-know / look-up / cut] | [why it belongs here] | [practice / explanation / job aid / excluded] | [ID/SME, date] |

- **Must-do:** actions and decisions learners need to practice.
- **Must-know:** information essential to performing those actions; keep it connected to the task.
- **Look-up:** information best accessed at the moment of need; teach locating or using it when relevant.
- **Cut:** duplicate, irrelevant, outdated, or out-of-scope content; retain an explicit reason and source reference.

Job aid candidates:

| Candidate | Task and point of use | Proposed format | Source | Update owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [name] | [when needed] | [checklist / decision table / reference] | [reference] | [role] |

Produce a separate cut list with item IDs and reasons. Confirm learner prerequisites, course duration, included and excluded tasks, and non-training dependencies before finalizing objectives.

## 4. SMART learning objectives

Start with: **By [approved learning milestone], [role] will be able to [observable work behavior and object].** Add conditions/resources and explicit criteria when they materially define performance; record assessment standards separately when appropriate. Use the [objective guidance](../skills/course-learning-design/references/learning-objectives.md) for terminal/enabling objectives, Bloom alignment, and learner-facing purpose statements.

| Objective ID | Gap/task/content references | Objective | Specific | Measurable | Achievable | Relevant | Time-bound | Approval |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [ID] | [references] | [draft] | [clear action and scope] | [observable evidence and criterion] | [feasible for learners, time, resources] | [link to training-relevant gap] | [course end, practice milestone, or agreed date] | [ID/SME, status] |

- Avoid “understand” or “know” alone. Use observable behavior at the cognitive level the task requires.
- Check achievability against prerequisites, available practice, course length, tools, and learner authority. Revise unrealistic scope rather than promising business results outside learner control.
- Do not invent accuracy percentages, speed requirements, or dates. A numeric criterion is not mandatory. Confirm any essential performance standards before objective/assessment approval, including a justified decision that no additional condition or numeric metric is needed.
- A time-bound learning milestone does not require a timed task. Add a speed criterion only when the work genuinely requires it and the SME confirms it.
- Link each objective to a planned practice and an assessment capable of eliciting that behavior.

## 5. Scenario-based learning plan

Before detailing scenarios, derive the overall course sequence using the [course map and strategy template](../skills/course-learning-design/references/course-map-template.md). Record how approved needs analysis, learner/environment constraints, SME scope, and SMART objectives justify each instructional choice. Review the map with the ID before production storyboarding.

For sales practice, scaffold from earlier guided, fixed-choice scenarios to later free-form AI role-play. The latter has no answer options or unsolicited suggested replies: learners compose their own contributions, the counterpart responds in context, and the final debrief addresses evidence from those actual inputs. Document the progression in the course map. Guided-practice feedback may appear per choice; AI role-play keeps evaluation hidden until the end, with hints available only on request.

Select one or more purposes: opening hook, case threaded across the course, decision practice, scenario assessment, or AI role-play. A hook introduces a relevant problem; later sections can revisit the case with new information and decisions rather than merely repeating its story.

| Scenario ID | Purpose and course placement | Objective | Incident/mistake source | Learner-visible setup and cues | Decision point |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [ID] | [hook / recurring case / practice / assessment] | [ID] | [SME-validated reference or labeled synthetic example] | [context, stakes, available information] | [choice required] |

| Decision ID | Choice | Mistake or valid reasoning represented | Consequence | Feedback explaining why | Next node/end |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [ID] | [option] | [SME reference] | [plausible outcome] | [reason and source/job aid] | [ID] |

All choices need consequences and explanatory feedback. Branch where outcomes meaningfully differ; allow recovery where appropriate. Check that all routes resolve, that no accidental loops exist, and that learners have enough information to decide. SME-confirm consequences and exceptions. Confirm whether feedback is immediate, delayed, or deferred until assessment submission.

For live AI role-play, use the [ID scenario template](role-play-id-template.md) and [criteria template](role-play-criteria-template.md). Its assessment stays private until the final debrief; only learner-requested hints appear during practice.

## 6. Assessment brief: confirm with the ID before generation

Use the project-local [assessment design skill](../skills/course-assessment-design/SKILL.md) for detailed format selection, stem/distractor construction, feedback, rubric, and full-set review. It does not replace the confirmation gate below.

| Objective ID | Exact behavior/aspect to assess | Cognitive level and evidence needed | Question count | Item format | Real-life scenario? | Approved source/example |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [ID] | [which part of the objective] | [what would demonstrate it] | [ID confirms] | [decision MCQ / task / other] | [yes/no; real anonymized or synthetic] | [reference] |

Also confirm purpose (practice or assessment), scoring/criteria if required, attempt rules, feedback and answer-release timing, and any randomization requirements. Present unanswered fields to the ID instead of silently selecting counts or formats. Approve the brief before generating items. Do not reuse a worked example with its answer exposed as evidence of independent application; use an appropriate variation when needed.

Each item records its ID, objective/aspect, prompt, options or expected response, correct answer and rationale, mistake behind each distractor, feedback per choice, and source reference. Use a suitable performance task when a selected-response item cannot demonstrate the objective.

Quality review:

- Coverage and counts match the confirmed brief; content was taught or supported by allowed resources.
- Scenarios assess the intended decision, using plausible mistakes and adequate cues rather than trivia or trick wording.
- Options are comparable in length, specificity, and tone. The correct choice must not consistently be longest or occupy the same position; review the full set, not just individual questions.
- Do not shorten a necessary explanation merely to balance length; separate rationale from the displayed option.
- Check grammatical clues, implausible distractors, ambiguous correct answers, and unsupported facts.
- Feedback explains each choice and its consequence, subject to the approved release timing.

## 7. Authoring brief and storyboard

Use the [storyboard skill](../skills/course-storyboard/SKILL.md) to apply the tool-specific contract below and manage ID feedback/versioned revisions.

Confirm authoring tool, delivery mode, target duration, narration needs, accessibility requirements, and output format with the ID. HTML is one possible output; it must not determine every storyboard format.

For **Storyline**, provide a slide-by-slide build specification. Repeat this block for each slide, with separate layer/state rows where needed:

| Field | Slide specification |
|---|---|
| Slide ID, title, type, duration | [stable ID; estimated duration, not invented task deadline] |
| Objective and source references | [objective, selected content, scenario node, source] |
| On-screen text | [exact learner-facing copy, button labels, instructions] |
| Visual/image description | [subject, setting, action, composition, relevant cues, style, placement, aspect ratio, continuity; usable as an image-generation brief] |
| Interactions | [learner action, object, trigger, condition, variable/state/layer changes, feedback, next destination] |
| Narration | [complete script; distinguish spoken text from build notes] |
| Accessibility | [meaningful alt text, decorative items, reading/focus order, keyboard operation, accessible alternatives, captions/transcript, contrast and reduced-motion requirements] |
| Navigation and branches | [previous/next, prerequisites, retries, scenario paths] |
| Review status | [facts/visual cues awaiting SME confirmation; ID approval] |

For **Rise**, use a linear storyboard organized by course → lesson → ordered content blocks. Do not force the content into slides or require Storyline-specific layers, triggers, and variables. Confirm the preferred level of detail with the ID; a continuous lesson script with clearly labeled block boundaries is acceptable.

For each lesson, record its ID, title, linked objectives, opening/hook, and summary/application. Repeat this block specification in the learner's reading order:

| Field | Rise block specification |
|---|---|
| Lesson ID, block ID, sequence | [stable references and position within the lesson] |
| Block purpose and proposed type | [teaching purpose; text, image, interaction, knowledge check, or other ID-approved format] |
| Objective and source references | [objective, selected content, scenario node, source] |
| Learner-facing content | [exact headings, body text, instructions, labels, and transition copy] |
| Visual/image description | [actionable image-generation brief, placement, and continuity with the case] |
| Interaction or decision | [prompt, choices/actions, consequences, explanatory feedback, retry behavior, and any scenario paths] |
| Media script, if used | [audio/video script, captions, and transcript; do not assume every block needs narration] |
| Accessibility | [alt text, meaningful order, accessible interaction alternatives, and media requirements] |
| Progression | [how this block connects to the next; any ID-approved completion requirement] |
| Review status | [SME factual checks and ID approval] |

Linear describes the storyboard's organization, not a requirement to remove decisions or local scenario branches. Document those paths inside the relevant block. Confirm that the proposed interaction can be built in the selected tool; flag anything requiring an embed or custom implementation for ID review rather than promising native support.

For both formats, image descriptions and alt text serve different purposes; provide both when relevant. Do not put essential decision information only in an inaccessible visual or drag interaction. Validate accessibility in the built output rather than treating storyboard notes as proof of conformance.

## 8. Review gates and traceability

Use the [cross-stage quality review skill](../skills/course-quality-review/SKILL.md) to identify gate-specific blockers, alignment gaps, and unverified checks. Its recommendation does not replace actual ID/SME approval or runtime verification.

First-release delivery is storyboard-first: ID feedback and revision → explicit approval of a version → generation of embeddable interactions. See the [evaluation and tracking plan](evaluation-and-tracking.md) for Kirkpatrick's four levels, planned xAPI/LRS instrumentation, and delivery boundaries. Plan outcome measures during diagnosis and design; collect and interpret them at the appropriate time.

Confirm diagnosis → SME workflow → selected scope → SMART objectives → assessment brief and scenario plan → tool-specific storyboard → build and evaluation. Allow revisions with their reason and affected references recorded.

Maintain: **gap ID → task/SME note → selected content → objective ID → practice/scenario → assessment item → Storyline slide or Rise lesson/block**. Flag unsupported objectives, missing practice, uncovered objective aspects, unapproved criteria, and unresolved factual claims before build.
