# AI role-play: ID scenario template

Status: draft specification for a planned feature. This template is reusable across sales, service, and other workplace conversations.

Fill in what you know or supply source materials. Leave unknowns explicit. AI should extract supported facts, propose fictional scenario details separately, and ask focused questions about gaps. It must not invent policies, product capabilities, or evaluation thresholds.

## 2. Situation and roles

Later-stage AI role-play uses free-form learner input throughout, like a real conversation. Do not display answer choices, suggested replies, or automatic response completions. The counterpart responds to the learner's actual input and conversation history. Assess meaning and observable behavior, not a scripted phrase match. Open input permits questions, statements, summaries, and confirmations; it does not require every utterance to be an open-ended question.

Earlier guided practice may use authored fixed-choice scenarios with consequences and explanatory feedback. Keep that mode distinct from AI role-play. Only an explicit learner request opens the separate hint coach during role-play; hints guide thinking rather than supplying a reply to copy. Formal personalized feedback remains in the final debrief and cites the learner's actual contributions.

- Context pack and interaction modes: [environment; dialogue, screen, spatial, or combination]

Prompt: What would a real person know at the start? What must the learner discover through the conversation?

## 5. Criteria and feedback

- Criteria: complete the [criteria template](role-play-criteria-template.md), linked to objective IDs.
- Hint policy: [ID to confirm; proposed default: direction first, more specific clue on request, no ready-made response]
- Hint use: record it; no automatic penalty. Any distinction between assisted and independent performance needs ID approval.
- Debrief format: [ID to confirm; proposed default: criterion status, cited evidence, explanation, next practice step; no overall score]

During practice, the counterpart remains in character and formal assessment stays hidden. The separate coach gives hints only when requested. Hint text itself is not evidence of learner performance.

## 6. Completion

There are two instructional completion paths:

1. The learner selects **Finish and debrief**, regardless of criteria coverage.
2. The private evaluator confirms **all configured criteria are met**, with evidence, after a complete exchange. The counterpart gives a natural closing response, then the debrief appears.

Partially met, not demonstrated, no opportunity, and uncertain judgments do not satisfy automatic completion. Technical interruptions are not successful completion; preserve a resumable record or label any partial review as interrupted.

## 7. Review before use

- ID/reviewer, version, decision, and date: [complete]
- Confirm source facts and fictional details are clearly separated.
- Confirm each criterion has an achievable opportunity, explicit evidence requirements, and a route through the scenario.
- Preview strong, partial, off-track, hint-assisted, and early-exit conversations.
- Check that neither the counterpart nor unsolicited feedback reveals the rubric or answers.
- Confirm a different scenario can use the same engine with different configuration.
