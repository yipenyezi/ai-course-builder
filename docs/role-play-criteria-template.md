# AI role-play: criteria template

Status: draft specification. The ID approves the criteria before learners use the scenario.

## Repeat for each criterion

| Field | ID input or AI-assisted draft |
|---|---|
| Criterion ID | [stable identifier] |
| Linked objective and gap | [objective ID; performance gap reference] |
| Observable behavior | [what the learner says or does; avoid labels such as “good communication” alone] |
| Opportunity | [where the scenario gives a fair opportunity to demonstrate it] |

| Partially met | [what is present and what is still missing] |
| Not demonstrated | [what counts as a missed opportunity or contrary behavior] |
| No opportunity | [when the interaction never enabled this behavior] |
| Examples | [met and unmet examples; illustrative, not mandatory wording] |
| Source | [approved material supporting the standard] |
| Hint | [direction, then a more specific clue; ID-approved] |

Prompt: What would you need to see or hear to conclude the learner can do this? Could a different valid response also meet it?

## Evaluation contract

- Later-stage role-play collects free-form learner contributions without predefined answers. Use actual input and full conversation context as evidence; recognize valid alternative wording and approaches. Do not confuse the earlier fixed-choice practice mode with this assessment evidence.

- Evaluate privately after complete exchanges using the conversation so far and approved scenario facts. Do not send the counterpart evaluative coaching or unmet-criteria prompts that would lead it to reveal answers.
- Base judgments on learner behavior, not keywords, counterpart agreement, or a successful commercial outcome alone.
- Record criterion ID, status, learner turn references and exact excerpts, reasoning, and any uncertainty. Keep learner statements distinct from coach hints and counterpart statements.
- Use statuses **met**, **partially met**, **not demonstrated**, and **no opportunity**. When evidence is ambiguous, record uncertainty and do not mark met until resolved.
- Reconsider earlier judgments if later behavior contradicts them; completion uses the current full record, not permanently accumulated checkmarks.
- Hint use has no automatic penalty. If the ID requires independent demonstration, define an opportunity to demonstrate it without assistance.
- Automatic completion requires every configured criterion to be met with sufficient evidence and no unresolved judgment. Otherwise the learner may continue or choose to finish.
- Keep all evaluation hidden during practice. At either instructional ending, assess the full record again and show the final debrief.

## Debrief draft for ID approval

| Criterion | Final status | Conversation evidence | Explanation | Suggested next step |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [ID] | [status] | [turn reference and excerpt, or no evidence] | [what was demonstrated or missing] | [specific practice suggestion] |

Include the ending reason and hints used. Distinguish missing evidence from evidence of an error. An early exit is not evidence that the learner lacks the skill. Overall scores and pass thresholds are not assumed; any such design requires explicit ID configuration.

## Synthetic example: sales discovery

- Criterion: clarify the business outcome before recommending a solution.
- Opportunity: the fictional customer requests AI for inventory forecasting.
- Met: the learner elicits the desired business change and confirms their understanding before making a recommendation.
- Partially met: the learner asks about goals but recommends without clarifying the response.
- Not demonstrated: the learner recommends immediately despite an available discovery opportunity.
- No opportunity: the session ends before the learner can respond to the opening.
- First hint: “What would you need to understand before recommending a solution?”

This example illustrates behavior-based judgment. It does not impose the same criterion on every scenario.
